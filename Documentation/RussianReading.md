# Russian reading and saved texts — 25 October 2026

## Behavior

Listen → Russian, above a generated passage, prepares a complete Russian translation of the intact English original and reads it with the user-selected Denis voice. This also works for Balanced passages: Balanced generation and lexical replacement remain unchanged, while the user explicitly authorized the existing 2B model for this optional translation task. Stop, pause and resume operate on the active audio clip.

Speech remains off by default. Constructing the app environment or opening a saved text loads no speech weights. Explicit Listen starts it; pause, Stop, normal completion, failure, leaving the reading view and app background/quit release the native voice/alignment models. A paused clip retains only its small audio buffer and timestamps for resume. Release operations are serialized before the next synthesis, and iOS audio focus is relinquished when inactive. Other app sections do not initialize these models.

The reader offers Mixed text and Russian views. Follow spoken words is optional: Russian highlights the current word in yellow; mixed text highlights the corresponding original-source sentence because English and Russian word order differ. Turning the option off clears both markers. Timing follows the audio player's position and survives pause/resume. Recognition can miss words: no estimated timings are invented for unmatched words. Below 60% alignment coverage, the reader reports that word highlighting is unavailable for that sentence and retains the sentence marker.

Save explicitly stores a passage snapshot, reversible replacement spans and any prepared translations in the local Readings library. The library supports opening, title search, renaming and deletion. Mac has a Readings sidebar item; both platforms have Saved readings on Generate. A saved passage can later be translated and that translation is added atomically to its record. Previously prepared Russian text is reused after relaunch. Audio is regenerated locally, never archived. Ordinary generated passages and their translations remain in process memory until the app closes; backgrounding alone does not delete them. Vocabulary, lists and FSRS history use their existing tables and migrations.

## Architecture and packaging

- Core/Narration defines translation requests, source sentence spans, validation, PCM/WAV audio and word alignment independently of SwiftUI. TargetReadingTranslator makes fresh, constrained sentence requests against TextGenerationService. Numeric-value and output-shape checks reject some errors, but do not establish semantic accuracy.
- ModeGenerationService routes readingTranslation and Aggressive to Qwen3.5-2B; normal Balanced generation remains Qwen3.5-0.8B. Models are unloaded before switching. The LLM is unloaded before speech synthesis/alignment, and the narrator is unloaded before generation. No two LLMs remain loaded together.
- PiperSpeechService serializes native TTS off the main thread, supports cancellation and validates voice assets. SpeechSynthesisService and language-code manifests provide the extension point for later languages. Only Russian is currently enabled.
- PassageNarrator keeps one sentence's WAV/PCM at a time, plays it with AVAudioPlayer and maps its real playback clock to word ranges. RussianSpeechAligner analyzes the synthesized PCM with an offline Russian transducer; it never records a microphone. BPE subword tokens are aligned monotonically against Unicode-safe source word spans, including repeated words and written-out numbers.
- Database migration v5_opt_in_saved_readings adds one independent table. Save/update validates the source and translation spans; adding translations is transactional and cannot resurrect a deleted reading. Earlier migration and study data are preserved.
- The iOS and Mac presentation sources remain separate. Each platform adds PassageReadingView and SavedReadingsView and connects its own AppEnvironment. Tapped-word pronunciation remains available and pauses full-text narration.
- Tools/narration_bootstrap.py restores checksum-pinned static Apple runtimes and Russian assets during build.sh. Only the int8 encoder/joiner, decoder and tokens required by alignment are packaged. The full production bundle is checked by Tools/verify_app_bundle.py. Runtime network access, accounts and model downloads are absent.

Assets and exact hashes are recorded in Resources/Speech/dependencies.json, ru-manifest.json and ru-alignment-manifest.json. Generated models/runtime binaries remain outside Git. Speech libraries are statically linked; the Mac app retains its sandbox and hardened library validation.

## Executed validation

- 100 Core and 5 inference Swift tests passed, alongside 17 Python build/data tests. New cases cover Russian request validation, numeric values, Unicode source coverage, Codable snapshots, PCM/WAV encoding, repeated/BPE/numeric alignment, opt-in saved-text persistence with vocabulary/review preservation, and default-off/cancellation/native-release behavior. Migration expectations include v5. A pre-existing daily SRS admission test now uses a fixed date to avoid crossing midnight during execution; scheduling code was unchanged.
- Real local 2B translation preserved the distinction between a financial bank and a river bank, the number 20, a concessive sentence and negation in four diagnostic sentences. The smaller model's trial omitted details, so it was not selected. These four examples are execution checks, not a broad linguistic evaluation.
- Native Denis synthesis produced about 10.7 seconds of audio for the selected Russian sample. The offline aligner matched 24 source words, 92.3% coverage. Mac trials observed roughly 0.5–2.3 seconds for synthesis depending on loading state. These are Mac observations, not iPhone benchmarks.
- Native Mac Release and iOS simulator builds passed actual bundle resource/hash checks. Mac signature validation passed. The installed Mac app generated an 83-word Aggressive passage; full Russian text, playback, a yellow spoken-word marker, mixed-view sentence marker, marker-off, pause/resume and stop were exercised. Saving and opening the passage worked; after a normal quit/relaunch its Russian translation reappeared and played without repeating translation.
- A real iOS Balanced run generated 269 words. Its original 13-sentence translation trial exposed truncated JSON and an English phrase left untranslated. JSON decoding errors now enter the bounded retry path. Russian-only character constraints, an explicit idiom/name translation prompt and a modest repetition penalty prevent the observed Chinese-token loop and English phrase. The same 13 sentences then completed on the native Mac model. The output still contains grammar and meaning errors; successful validation is not linguistic certification.
- The final installed Mac build played its saved translation, paused/resumed after native model release, stopped on navigation to Study and remained idle on returning to the text.
- The final iOS simulator build opened the saved passage and played Denis audio. A yellow spoken-word marker was visually verified on the first sentence; pause/resume and navigation to Study were exercised. This audio check used the actual Mac-generated 13-sentence translation copied only into an isolated test library. The entire corrected translation was not regenerated on the simulator CPU. The simulator was subsequently shut down; its normal library was never replaced by the test data.

Build/smoke logs are under the ignored .bootstrap/logs directory: russian-reading-mac-final.log, russian-reading-ios-final.log, russian-reading-final-tests.log, russian-reading-regression-final.log, russian-translation-2b-smoke.log and the final-assets JSON reports. The installed Mac library includes the generated Psychology sample used for this check.

## Limits

Physical iPhone 13 inference speed, memory, thermal behavior and audio quality were not measured for this change. Simulator/Mac success does not establish those properties. Translation may omit or distort details despite structural validation; small-model grammar and pronunciation of names/rare words need human review. ASR timing is approximate and some words remain unmarked. Inter-sentence synthesis/alignment can create a short pause; a full passage's audio is not prefetched into memory. Fresh-install airplane-mode testing on the physical phone remains pending. The next useful check is listening to saved passages on that phone and reviewing translation fidelity and marker timing.
